---
title: Shared watches
description: One watch per kind and label selector, shared by every controller and store read (weebo-kit-runtime::shared).
---

Without them, each controller's `Controller::watches` opens its own
watch of a referenced kind, and every one of them LISTs when the
controllers start, right after the lease is taken. `weebo_kit_runtime::shared`
(v0.20.0, label selectors in v0.21.0) opens **one watch per kind and label
selector** for the whole binary. Measured in the five operators: forgejo
went from 37 watches to 15, batlehub 16 → 9, angos 12 → 7, stalwart
15 → 8, authentik 15 → 12.

## The pieces

- `SharedWatch<K>`: a reflector whose changes are broadcast to
  subscribers. `store()` for reads, `controller()` for the primary kind
  (same triggers and concurrency as `controller::controller`),
  `touched()` for a referenced kind. A subscriber gets the whole store
  once listed, then every change, and re-reads the store if it lagged.
- `SharedWatches`: the binary's watches, indexed by type and selector.
  `spawn(api, &scope, &health, name)` opens the watch of a kind through
  `scope`'s selector unless it exists (through
  `scope.without_selector()`: every object); `get::<K>()` (the first
  opened), `get_in::<K>(&scope)` (the one of a selector),
  `controller(api, &scope)`, `touched::<K>(&scope, &client)`,
  `wait_listed(timeout)`.
- `WatchesIn::watches_in(&watches, &scope, &client, map)` on a
  `Controller`: watch a referenced kind through the shared watch of the
  scope's selector, else the one of every object, else its own watch;
  skipped for a cluster-scoped kind when the operator watches none.
  `watches_in_filtered(…, predicate, map)` re-triggers only on the
  changes a kube `Predicate` sees.
- `LISTED_TIMEOUT` (30 s).

`controller(api, &scope)` of `SharedWatches` only reuses the watch of
the scope's own selector — never a wider one, which would reconcile
objects outside the scope.

## Wiring it (as the template does)

1. Spawn the watches **before** campaigning for the lease (the
   template's `controller::spawn_watches`): a standby replica keeps its
   stores warm.
2. Hand their stores to `Instances` and the other readers.
3. Wait for them to list, bounded, then campaign:

   ```rust
   ctx.watches.wait_listed(LISTED_TIMEOUT).await;
   leader::acquire_leadership(&client, holder_id, DEFAULT_LEASE_NAME).await
   ```

   A watch that can't list is reported by `/livez`, not waited for.
4. Build each controller with `ctx.watches.controller(api, &ctx.scope)`
   and its referenced kinds with `watches_in`.

`controller::controller` stays as it was: an operator adopts shared
watches when it wants. `task perf` validates the move (idle requests per
minute within `Budget::small_cluster()`).
